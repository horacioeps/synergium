We identify international collaborators, contact them and arrange the first meetings. The work runs from understanding the group to sitting both sides down.

Services

Partner search

We find groups that complement your science, contact them and set up the first meetings. You receive a ranked list — typically 15–20 organisations — with a profile and contacts for each, plus the meetings that can be arranged.

Coordinator search

When the gap is a coordinator with EU-project experience, not only a scientific partner. A shortlist of groups that can lead, with their consortium history and how to open the conversation.

Call fit

Whether a given call is a credible fit for your group or institution, and in what role. A short written assessment: yes, conditional, or no — and what would need to change.

Partner Snapshot

A first shortlist before a longer assignment: three candidate files in 7–10 days. Each file covers the science, the EU-project history, the fit, and how to open. One introduction is included.

How

  1. Profile. An initial meeting and a definition of the collaborator you need.
  2. Mapping. A ranked list of groups or organisations, with a profile and contacts.
  3. Contact. Introduction, follow-up and first meetings. We join the first meeting.

A full partner search usually takes about twelve weeks, with an update every two weeks. You receive the list, a short process summary and the meetings that could be arranged. A Partner Snapshot uses the same filters, cut to three candidates, in 7–10 days.

We do not write the scientific part of a proposal and we do not guarantee a grant.

Who it is for

Institutions and project offices that need a map of partners. Research groups with an open call, or with the same co-authors as always. European consultancies that need partners outside their usual network.

Who we are

A team used to setting up scientific collaborations. Part of the team is based in Spain; we work with institutions in several regions.
